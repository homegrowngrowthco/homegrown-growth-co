Most growth problems are not traffic problems. They are leak problems. Demand comes in, and somewhere between the first click and the renewal, it quietly drains out. A revenue engine that compounds is one where each part feeds the next instead of fighting it.

## Start with the leaks, not the funnel

Before adding more demand, find where the current demand is being lost. Map the path a customer actually takes, then look for the steps where people drop, stall, or get handed off and never picked back up. Those handoffs are usually where the money is.

A few common ones:

- Leads that come in but never get a timely first touch.
- Deals that go dark because nobody owns the follow-up.
- Customers who churn for reasons that were visible months earlier.

## Make the system do the remembering

People forget. Systems do not. The highest-leverage work is usually moving a fragile, human-memory process into something the tooling enforces: a routing rule, an automated reminder, a report that surfaces the at-risk accounts before they leave.

## Measure what compounds

Pick the few metrics that, when they improve, pull everything else up with them. Then instrument them so they are visible without anyone running a manual export. When the numbers are trustworthy and in front of you, the next decision gets easier.

That is the whole idea: each fix makes the next one cheaper. The engine compounds.
